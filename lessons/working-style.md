<h2>My Working Style</h2>
<p class="date">2026/11/25</p>

<p>For my future friends, so you can get to know me a little before we work together.</p>

<p>I like things short and simple. I prefer charts, formulas, and animations. It took me a long time to break my habit of writing long blocks of text, though this page still has quite a lot of words.</p>

<h3>The kind of request I like</h3>

<p>A good request looks like this: “Shiqin, can you take the xxx module?” Then it makes these points clear:</p>

<table class="ws">
<tr><th>Item</th><th>What to say</th></tr>
<tr><td>Input</td><td>The data type</td></tr>
<tr><td>Output</td><td>The result you need</td></tr>
<tr><td>Data</td><td>The data and code repos for the project, with access granted</td></tr>
<tr><td>Position</td><td>The upstream and downstream modules I connect to, and who owns each one</td></tr>
<tr><td>History</td><td>Whether earlier versions of this module exist</td></tr>
<tr><td>Limits</td><td>Any special requirements, such as safety</td></tr>
<tr><td>Goal</td><td>What the final result should look like</td></tr>
<tr><td>Deadline</td><td>When it is due</td></tr>
<tr><td>Checkpoint</td><td>Send a version when half the time is left, or check in with me at a set milestone</td></tr>
</table>

<h3>How I communicate</h3>

<ul>
<li>I reply to 90% of work messages and project questions within 4 hours, late nights included. For urgent projects, I am on call at any time.</li>
<li>I prefer 4 hours of focused work plus 4 hours of rest to 8 hours at medium speed.</li>
<li>I like to cut the work into small steps and talk often, in short cycles, so we all stay on the same page.</li>
<li>Unless there is a special reason, everyone on the project gets the same message.</li>
<li>I try to be proactive, and I hope the people I work with are a little proactive too. Let’s push things forward happily, and cheer each other on. After all, people are animals that love encouragement. But I fully respect that everyone has their own style!</li>
<li>If something comes up and you cannot finish your part, I am happy to take it over. Please tell me early. If the work is truly beyond what I know, I will at least give you another way to solve it.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Working together</h3>

<p><b>With classmates and friends at a company or on a project:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>We keep one shared document. It says what each person does, which code repo it is in, and who owns it.</li>
<li>The document also sets the rough code structure and the possible risks, such as safety checks and duplicate code.</li>
<li>Tasks are short and clear. We keep emotions out of them, and we do not take on more than we or others can do. It depends on the project: for an urgent one, I believe the learning happens in normal times, and on the project we just deliver.</li>
<li>We meet twice a week to talk about progress. During the work itself, I find meetings less efficient than commenting directly in a shared document (such as Feishu or Google Docs). So we meet when the text is not clear. The comments also become review material for the weekly meeting.</li>
<li>What matters most to me: I do not let myself work together based only on my own ideas. Everyone’s time and strengths are precious. The team must make sure each person can finish their part, and also grow and get what they need.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>With my advisor:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Each week I prepare slides. They show the progress of each proposition with drawings and charts. At the end, I add my own reflection, so my advisor can suggest how to improve.</li>
</ul>

<p><b>With AI:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>I keep a work tree with AI in Obsidian. For example, once I have set the propositions and hypotheses for memory, I hand off jobs like pulling code from GitHub and downloading databases to AI, in parallel. Then I go on to look at other things.</li>
</ul>
